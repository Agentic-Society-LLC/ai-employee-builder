# Platforms: where the employee lives

Five platforms, two tiers. Researched against official docs on 2026-10-14 and re-verified the same day; the few things marked **VERIFY** could not be confirmed from a primary source and should be checked in the product before you rely on them. Press-only facts are marked **(press)**. Products in this space change monthly: when a screen does not match, trust the screen, tell the CEO, and re-derive the steps.

| Platform | Made by | Tier | Who it is for | Runs where | Claude models? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Muse** | Meta | simple | non-technical, consumer and small business; limited-availability rollout | Meta's cloud (one computer per Muse) | No (Muse Spark) |
| **Grok** (Grok Bot) | xAI-branded, operated and billed by Cursor | simple | business users, sales / ads / finance / chief-of-staff jobs | xAI's cloud (one computer per account, a screen per Bot) | No (Cursor-managed mix) |
| **Dots** | OpenAI | simple | ChatGPT Pro / Business / Enterprise users | OpenAI's cloud | No (GPT-6 Astra) |
| **Hermes** (Hermes Agent) | Nous Research, open source | advanced | technical or power users, CLI first | your laptop, a VPS, or a sandbox | **Yes** (API key, Claude Max, Opus) |
| **OpenClaw** | OpenClaw Foundation, open source | advanced | technical or power users | your Mac, Linux or Windows box, or a VPS | **Yes** (API key, Claude CLI reuse, Opus) |

## Routing (Phase 5.1 and 5.2)

The rule is Austin's: **tech level 1 to 6, or no intention to wire APIs, MCPs or CLIs → simple tier, Grok by default. Tech level 7 or higher and a real intention to build on APIs, MCPs and advanced workflows → advanced tier, Hermes by default.** Then pick within the tier from what the interview already told you, and name the runner-up:

**Simple tier**
- **Grok** when: the job is a business job (sales, paid media, finance, operations), the CEO may want several Bots as a team, they already pay for a Cursor plan, SuperGrok or X Premium+ (Grok Bot is included with those, metered on a Cursor account), or they said "Grok". The default.
- **Muse** when: the CEO is at tech level 1 to 3, lives in Gmail / Calendar / WhatsApp / iMessage on a Mac, wants free to start, and can get in (Muse is in limited testing and not in every location). Muse lets you edit `Soul.md`, `Identity.md` and `Memory.md` in the app, so the packet maps straight across, and its small-business connectors cover Stripe, QuickBooks, HighLevel, Slack, Notion, Shopify, Granola and Zoom.
- **Dots** when: the CEO already pays for ChatGPT Pro (from $100/month), Business Premium or Enterprise (beta, admin-enabled) and the team lives in ChatGPT, Slack or Microsoft Teams. Not on Free / Go / Plus, and Pro dots are not in the EEA, UK or Switzerland at launch.

**Advanced tier**
- **Hermes** when: the CEO wants Claude Opus as the model, many messaging surfaces (Telegram, Slack, WhatsApp, Signal, email, SMS, iMessage via a bridge), real webhooks, cron with cron syntax, and a skills folder. The default.
- **OpenClaw** when: the CEO wants it on their own Mac with native iMessage, the largest skill catalog (ClawHub), the dashboard, or already uses Claude Code / Codex logins it can reuse. Also right when the CEO already runs OpenClaw.
- **Tie-break:** both fit most technical CEOs. Pick Hermes unless one of these is true: they already run OpenClaw, they want native iMessage on a signed-in Mac, or they want a web dashboard over a CLI. Say which of those decided it.

Always state the reason in one line and let the CEO override. Record `platform.choice`, `tier`, `reason`, `runnerUp`, `techLevel` in the tracker. If the choice is a hosted platform, note in the contract that the model is the vendor's and Opus 5.5 is the model for **this build session**, not for the employee's runtime.

## The model for the build

Tell the CEO at the start: this interview and build runs best on **Claude Opus 5.5** because it holds the whole contract, the packet and the platform steps in its head at once. In Claude Code: `/model` → Opus 5.5. In the Claude app: the model picker at the top of the chat. In Cowork: the model menu. If they cannot switch, carry on; say so once and do not mention it again.

## Muse (Meta)

- **What:** Meta's personal AI agent (muse.ai on the web, iPhone and Android apps, a Mac app) that takes actions with your direction; small-business features since Sept 29 2026. Hosted. **Free with a usage limit; Power $20/month (500M Muse tokens a week); Maximum $100/month (3B a week)**; "in limited testing and not available in all locations yet" (Meta Help Center). Powered by Meta's Muse Spark model family (press).
- **Install:** (1) open muse.ai or install the iPhone or Android app; (2) sign in with a Meta Account (Facebook, Instagram, email or phone); (3) Muse opens on Chat; type the first request. Docs: ai.meta.com/muse and the Meta Help Center's Muse section.
- **Where the packet goes:** tap the Assistant icon → Identity / Memory. `Soul.md`, `Identity.md` and `Memory.md` are editable in-app. Paste SOUL.md into Soul.md, IDENTITY.md into Identity.md, and the rendered instructions (packet.md, "Rendering") below the introduction in Soul.md. Name, avatar, tagline and tone are set in chat or settings. Skills are Meta-built; you cannot add your own.
- **Routines:** natural language only: "Every Monday at 9am, send me the at-risk client list." Muse confirms; view and change under Assistant → Upcoming; "times are based on your time zone"; recurring tasks run until cancelled. There is **no pause** for a routine: cancel or reschedule. DST behaviour is not documented: watch the first run after a clock change.
- **Triggers:** no user-facing webhooks. Some connectors push updates (calendar changes) and say so when connected. For "when Stripe gets a new customer", the Stripe connector plus a polling routine, or the Zapier route if it is listed in the CEO's Settings → Connectors.
- **Tools:** Settings → Connectors → Connect, or say "Connect my Gmail"; per-connector read-only vs act permissions; Facebook / Instagram / Threads connect through Accounts Center; Gmail and Google Workspace built in. **Small-business connectors (Meta newsroom):** Asana, Box, Canva, Dropbox, Figma, Granola, HighLevel, Intuit QuickBooks, Klaviyo, Lovable, Notion, Shopify, Slack, Stripe, Zoom, plus Facebook and Instagram business accounts; "dozens" more in the app's list. **Custom Connectors**: Muse builds one from a service's API; credentials go in the Secure Credentials Store (Meta does not review them). "Nothing publishes, sends, or spends without your approval." No MCP configuration is documented (**VERIFY**: do not promise it). On a Mac, iMessage, Notes, Reminders, Mail and Calendar can be set Off / Read only / Read and interact per app.
- **Talk to it on:** the apps, the web, WhatsApp (Meta's launch copy as quoted by press; no Help Center page yet). Not Telegram, Slack or Discord.
- **Stop button:** there is no pause switch for a routine. The honest stop, in order: Settings → Permissions → "Always ask" on every action; disconnect the connectors the routine uses; cancel the routine. On a live browser task, "Stop the task" or "Take control of the browser". Write that sequence in the runbook as the stop button and have the CEO rehearse it. Settings → Data Controls → Reset Muse deletes everything (irreversible).
- **Memory:** Memory.md in-app, or ask it to forget. "Download your Muse data" under Data controls.

## Grok (xAI Grok Bot)

- **What:** "your team of always-on agents", beta since Aug 2026. xAI-branded, **operated by Cursor**: "the computers Bots work on run in Cursor's cloud", "Grok Bot uses your Cursor account", and usage is metered on the Cursor account. Included with every paid individual Cursor plan and Cursor Teams; a SuperGrok, SuperGrok Plus / Heavy or X Premium+ subscription can be linked as a usage grant (billed by xAI or X, not stackable). Weekly usage resets; optional on-demand overflow. No per-user model picker: each task is routed to "the backend model most likely to deliver the best outcome" (xAI or third-party). Docs: docs.x.ai/grok-bot and cursor.com/help/grok-bot.
- **Install:** (1) install the desktop app (macOS, Windows, Linux) or mobile app; (2) Sign in, finish authentication in the browser; (3) New → Create new Bot, describe its job, grant access as it asks.
- **Where the packet goes:** the Bot's **description** holds the lasting rules: paste the rendered instructions there (SOUL first, then AGENTS, then USER, then the first job). Edit Profile sets the name and avatar. Each recurring job becomes a **skill**: ask the Bot to "save this as a skill" or use Teach a task; invoke with `/`; manage under Connect apps → Your plugins → Manage plugins and skills. One skill per `skills/<job>.md`.
- **Routines:** tell the Bot in plain words ("Every weekday at 7:00 AM, run the morning brief skill and prepare it for Dana"). It confirms the schedule and time zone and shows the next run. Minimum 5 minutes apart, up to 50 per Bot. Set the Bot's time zone first: Settings → Bot → Timezone. Pause or enable under View conversation details → Routines. Routines may auto-pause if the owner is away and ignores the keep-running prompt; put that in the runbook. No cron syntax; DST undocumented.
- **A routine is personal.** "A routine belongs to whoever set it up with the Bot, in their own chat. It runs as them, reports in their chat, and only they can see or change it." So the owner's own account is the only place the routine can be paused. If the person who should notice misbehaviour is not the owner (an ops lead), the stop button is "they message the owner, who pauses it", and the contract says so.
- **Sending vs drafting:** the documented flow is a draft: "when a Bot prepares an email or Slack message for you, the conversation can show an editable draft. Review the recipients and body, then choose Send email or Send message, or Discard." Unattended sends exist only through an "Always allow" approval rule. For a first job, plan the delivery as **the Bot's own conversation thread plus a draft in the owner's mailbox**, and leave "Always allow" off until promotion.
- **Triggers:** "Cursor account integrations can start a routine from an event, such as a Slack message or a GitHub notification"; tagging @bot on X hands a post to the Bot. No inbound webhook is documented (**VERIFY**; vendor pages claim one). For Stripe-style events, poll on a routine.
- **Tools:** Connect apps → Add → sign in in the browser; plugins come from the Cursor plugin catalog, so **Fathom, Zoom and Granola are all available** (Cursor Marketplace: Fathom "search meetings and pull transcripts and summaries"; Zoom authorization from the Grok Bot desktop app currently fails with error 4700, use the web). `@` attaches a connector to a task. **MCP:** yes, remote HTTPS or command servers (a local server on the CEO's machine cannot be reached from the cloud computer; Enterprise can allow-list). **Secrets:** the "secure secret request" (the value is masked and never enters the conversation) and Team Bot secrets (up to 25, encrypted); the CEO takes over the screen for passwords and 2FA, never the Bot.
- **Talk to it on:** the desktop and mobile apps, voice, group chats of several Bots, Slack (a Team Bot's Slack app), @bot on X. No official Telegram, iMessage or WhatsApp channel.
- **Stop button:** "Send a direct 'Stop now' message when work should end immediately" (it does not undo completed actions). Pause the routine under Routines. Delete the Bot; reset the Bot's computer (last resort). Hiding a Bot does not pause it. Runbook: "pause every routine under Routines; if it is still acting, send 'Stop now', then delete the Bot."
- **Memory:** per-Bot preferences and summaries, corrected in chat; shared files under `/workspace` on the cloud computer. No edit screen.

## Dots (OpenAI)

- **What:** "an always-on agent in ChatGPT that can help you take a project from an idea to work you can use … it can keep making progress between conversations" (OpenAI Help Center). Powered by GPT-6 Astra, with its own cloud computer. **The first dot is included with ChatGPT Pro (from $100/month, three usage tiers) and Business Premium (not priced on the public page); Enterprise, Edu and Healthcare can try the beta when an admin turns it on (off by default).** Not on Free, Go or Plus; Pro dots are not in the EEA, Switzerland or the UK at launch. Docs: help.openai.com ("Getting started with your dot") and learn.chatgpt.com/docs/dots.
- **Install:** (1) open ChatGPT in the mobile app, the desktop app or desktop web (not mobile web) and open Your dot; (2) follow the onboarding prompts, name it (default handle `@yourname-dot`); (3) on desktop, connect apps and messaging channels (Slack, Microsoft Teams; texting is a limited US beta for Pro).
- **Where the packet goes:** no files. Paste the rendered instructions as the first message and ask it to adopt them as its standing instructions; then Settings → Personalization → Custom rules sets one of four behaviours per action: take action without asking, take action when you say so, ask before taking action, hand off to you. Map AGENTS.md's approval boundary onto those four; rules cannot remove mandatory confirmations.
- **Routines:** ask for a fixed schedule in plain words, including "the time zone and any end date" ("each weekday at 9 AM Pacific"); it appears under Scheduled in the dot's profile. By default a dot self-paces its follow-ups; for an employee insist on fixed schedules. DST undocumented. Note: **Pause does not cancel future scheduled runs**; cancel them under Scheduled.
- **Triggers:** reacts to events when the connected service supports them; proactive work between prompts is read-only research. No webhook. Poll on a routine for everything else.
- **Tools:** the Plugins tab, shared with ChatGPT and Codex; "plugins can include skills and MCP servers" (some need extra setup or sign-in); optional connection to the CEO's own computer.
- **Talk to it on:** ChatGPT desktop, web and mobile, voice calls, Slack, Microsoft Teams (Enterprise admins must enable "Add dots to Slack and Microsoft Teams"); iMessage / RCS / WhatsApp texting is a limited beta (US Pro) and unavailable for Enterprise at launch.
- **Stop button:** Pause stops the current main task; Resume continues; scheduled and delegated work is cancelled under Activity or Scheduled. Reset deletes conversations, memories and scheduled tasks; deleting the dot is irreversible.
- **Memory:** the dot keeps its own notes, separate from ChatGPT memory; individual dot memories cannot be viewed or deleted ("to delete your dot's own saved memories, you need to delete your dot"). So MEMORY.md in the packet is the reviewable store, and the CEO corrects the dot in conversation.

## Hermes (Hermes Agent, Nous Research)

- **What:** "the self-improving AI agent", MIT-licensed, free; you pay a model provider. CLI first, with a desktop app for Windows and Apple-silicon Macs. Runs on a laptop, a VPS as a systemd service, or sandboxes (Docker, SSH, Daytona, Modal, Vercel Sandbox). Python 3.14. Docs: hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs · GitHub: NousResearch/hermes-agent.
- **Install:** (1) `curl -fsSL https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/install.sh | bash` (Windows: `iex (irm https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/install.ps1)`); (2) `source ~/.zshrc`; (3) `hermes`, then `hermes model` to pick the provider. Messaging: `hermes gateway setup`.
- **Claude:** `hermes config set model anthropic/claude-opus-5-5` with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, or Claude Max OAuth (Max plan with extra usage credits; Pro cannot), or a setup token.
- **Where the packet goes:** `~/.hermes/SOUL.md` is read verbatim every session as slot one of the system prompt (SOUL.md goes here, introduction first). `AGENTS.md` in the project folder (AGENTS.md goes here). `~/.hermes/memories/USER.md` (1,375 chars) and `MEMORY.md` (2,200 chars) are snapshotted into every session (a compressed USER.md goes here; the full one stays in the packet). Skills: `~/.hermes/skills/<category>/<job>/SKILL.md` with `name` and `description` frontmatter, optional `references/`, `templates/`, `scripts/`; one per `skills/<job>.md`. Hermes can import an OpenClaw workspace.
- **Time zone first:** set `timezone: "America/Chicago"` in `~/.hermes/config.yaml` (or `HERMES_TIMEZONE`). It "affects cron scheduling and the time injected into the system prompt". There is **no per-job time-zone flag**, and with the zone left empty an open bug makes jobs fire an hour off after a clock change (GitHub issue 119969), so a named IANA zone is not optional. `hermes doctor` flags a bad name.
- **Routines:** `hermes cron create "weekdays at 7am" "Run the morning brief" --name "Morning brief" --deliver telegram`, or in chat `/cron add "every monday 9am" "…"`. Accepts plain words, intervals, cron expressions (`0 7 * * 1-5`) and ISO timestamps. Jobs in `~/.hermes/cron/jobs.json`, output in `~/.hermes/cron/output/<job>/`; the gateway must be running (60-second tick). **A job can run a script instead of an agent turn:** `--script runtime/run.mjs --no-agent` (with `--interpreter` if needed), which is exactly where the four floor controls belong (`go-live.md`, Stage 4). Also `--paused`, `--provider`, `--model`, `--deliver`.
- **Triggers:** a webhook platform in the gateway. In `~/.hermes/.env`: `WEBHOOK_ENABLED=true`, `WEBHOOK_PORT=8644`, `WEBHOOK_SECRET=…`. Then `hermes webhook subscribe stripe-new-customer --events "customer.created" --prompt "New customer: {data.object.email}. Run the onboarding skill, human-first." --deliver telegram --deliver-chat-id <id>` gives `http://host:8644/webhooks/stripe-new-customer` with HMAC verification. `hermes webhook list | test | remove`. Put Hookdeck or similar in front if the box is not reachable from the internet.
- **Tools:** MCP via `mcp_servers:` in `~/.hermes/config.yaml` (stdio `command`/`args`, or `url` plus `headers`, with `${VAR}` resolved from `.env`; `auth: oauth` supported), `hermes mcp add`, or `hermes mcp install <name>` from a curated catalog. Composio and Zapier are not built in; both publish MCP endpoints that fit `mcp_servers`: Composio `https://backend.composio.dev/v3/mcp/<server-id>?user_id=<user>` with header `x-api-key`, or `https://connect.composio.dev/mcp` with `x-consumer-api-key`; Zapier `https://mcp.zapier.com/api/v1/connect` with `Authorization: Bearer <connection token>`.
- **Where secrets go:** `~/.hermes/.env` (`hermes config set NAME value` writes UPPER_SNAKE names there; `hermes secrets` can pull from Bitwarden). A script run by cron gets a sanitised environment, so a secret the script needs must be declared: `terminal.env_passthrough: [MY_SERVICE_TOKEN]` in `config.yaml`, value in `.env`. Never in SOUL.md or a skill.
- **Where the controls live:** the cron job runs `runtime/run.mjs` with `--script --no-agent`; the claim, ceiling, run record and stop button are in that script, which calls the model itself for the judgement step.
- **Talk to it on:** CLI, Telegram, Discord, Slack, WhatsApp, Signal, Matrix, Mattermost, email, SMS, Microsoft Teams, Google Chat, LINE, iMessage via BlueBubbles or Photon, Home Assistant.
- **Stop button:** one job: `hermes cron pause <job>` / `hermes cron resume <job>`. One channel without a restart: `/platform pause telegram` / `/platform resume telegram`. Everything: `hermes gateway stop`. Current turn: `/stop`. The employee's stop button is `hermes cron pause` for each of its jobs; list them in the runbook.
- **Cost:** `hermes insights` aggregates token and cost analytics; a headless run with `--usage-file report.json` reports `estimated_cost_usd`. `hermes cron runs` records status and errors, not dollars, so the run record's cost field comes from the script's own token count.
- **Memory:** `MEMORY.md` and `USER.md` under `~/.hermes/memories/`, written by the agent's memory tool; set `memory.write_approval: true` so writes queue for `/memory pending | approve | reject`. That is the arbitrate verb, built in. Sessions in `~/.hermes/state.db` with full-text search.

## OpenClaw

- **What:** open-source assistant that runs on your own computer and connects to the chat apps you already use. MIT, developed by the OpenClaw Foundation (formerly Clawdbot and Moltbot). Free; you pay the model provider. Node.js 24.16+. Runs as a LaunchAgent, systemd user unit or Scheduled Task, or on a VPS. Docs: docs.openclaw.ai · GitHub: openclaw/openclaw.
- **Install:** (1) `curl -fsSL https://openclaw.ai/install.sh | bash` (Windows: `iwr -useb https://openclaw.ai/install.ps1 | iex`, or `npx openclaw@latest`); (2) the onboarding wizard (`openclaw onboard --classic` for step by step; needs a Claude Code or Codex login, or an API key); (3) Ctrl-C the foreground Gateway and `openclaw gateway install`. Then `openclaw dashboard` (port 18789).
- **Claude:** provider `anthropic` with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` (`openclaw onboard --auth-choice apiKey`), reuse of the Claude CLI login, or a setup token. Model ref `anthropic/claude-opus-5-5`; set with `openclaw models set anthropic/claude-opus-5-5` or `/model`.
- **Where the packet goes:** the workspace, default `~/.openclaw/workspace` (keep it in a private git repo; never commit secrets; `~/.openclaw/` itself stays out). Loaded every session: `AGENTS.md` (operating instructions; its `## Tools` section is where TOOLS.md's table goes, since the standalone TOOLS.md is no longer read), `SOUL.md`, `USER.md` (4,000-char budget). `IDENTITY.md` is created in the first-run bootstrap ritual (paste the packet's IDENTITY.md over it). `MEMORY.md` is read in the main private session only; `memory/YYYY-MM-DD.md` daily notes; optional `DREAMS.md`. Skills: `<workspace>/skills/<job>/SKILL.md` with `name`/`description` frontmatter (`openclaw skills install @owner/<slug>` from ClawHub). **`HEARTBEAT.md` is legacy and not read**; the packet's `ROUTINES.md` is documentation, and the routines themselves are registered with the command below. Bootstrap files are truncated at 20,000 chars each, 60,000 total: keep the packet short.
- **Routines:** `openclaw automations add --name "Morning brief" --cron "0 7 * * 1-5" --tz "America/Chicago" --message "Run the morning-brief skill and deliver it to Dana." --session isolated` (`openclaw cron` is an alias). Kinds: `--at`, `--every`, `--cron` (5 or 6 fields, parsed by croner), `--on-exit`, `--stream-command`. **Always pass `--tz`** (an IANA zone; it is valid with `--cron` and `--at`, not with `--every`); without it cron uses the host's zone and bare timestamps are UTC. With a zone, croner handles clock changes: a job scheduled inside a spring-forward gap is skipped, one inside a fall-back overlap runs once. Top-of-hour jobs are staggered up to 5 minutes unless `--exact`. Manage: `enable | disable | edit | run | remove | list | runs`; `run` is the manual trigger for Stage 4. A job can carry a **command payload** instead of a message, with `--command-env KEY=VALUE` for what it needs: that is where the runtime script runs. Heartbeat: `agents.defaults.heartbeat.every: "30m"` by default (`"0m"` disables), with `activeHours` and a `timezone`.
- **Triggers:** inbound hooks. Config `hooks.enabled: true`, `hooks.token`, `hooks.path: "/hooks"`. `POST /hooks/agent` with `message`, `deliver` and an `Idempotency-Key` header (that header is the per-event claim); `POST /hooks/wake`; `POST /hooks/<name>` resolved through `hooks.mappings` (match on path or source, optional transform); a `gmail` preset for Pub/Sub. Example: `curl -X POST http://127.0.0.1:18789/hooks/agent -H "Authorization: Bearer $HOOK_TOKEN" -H "Idempotency-Key: cus_123" -H "Content-Type: application/json" -d '{"message":"New Stripe customer cus_123: run onboarding, human-first.","deliver":false}'`. Also `--trigger-script ./watch.js` on an `every` schedule that fires only when the script returns `fire: true` (a polling trigger in one file).
- **Tools:** MCP client: `openclaw mcp add <name> --url https://… --transport streamable-http --header "Authorization: Bearer …" --include 'search,read_*'` (or `--auth oauth`), or stdio with `--command` / `--arg`; config under `mcp.servers`; `openclaw mcp doctor <name> --probe`; never commit a literal token. Skills, plugins, `exec`. Composio and Zapier are not built in: Composio publishes `@composio/openclaw-plugin` and the endpoint `https://connect.composio.dev/mcp` (header `x-consumer-api-key`); Zapier's own OpenClaw page uses mcporter with `https://mcp.zapier.com/api/v1/connect`. The `--include` allow-list is how you make a connection read-only.
- **Where secrets go:** `~/.openclaw/.env` (global; also a `.env` in the Gateway's working directory, and non-secret values under `env.vars` in the config), never the workspace. Precedence: the Gateway process env, then cwd `.env`, then `~/.openclaw/.env`, then `env.vars`; existing values are never overridden. A cron command payload can also pass `--command-env KEY=VALUE`.
- **Where the controls live:** the automation runs `runtime/run.mjs` as a command payload (or, if you prefer an agent turn, `--message "run node <packet>/runtime/run.mjs --job <job>"` with `--session isolated`); the claim, ceiling, run record and stop button are in that script.
- **Talk to it on:** WebChat (Control UI), Telegram and X bundled; official plugins for iMessage (via `imsg` on a signed-in Mac), Slack, Discord, Microsoft Teams, Signal, WhatsApp, Google Chat, Matrix, Mattermost, SMS, LINE, IRC.
- **Stop button:** one job: `openclaw automations disable <jobId>`. Everything, pausable: `openclaw gateway suspend` (the ready output prints the suspension id, the lease expiry and the matching resume command) → `openclaw gateway resume <suspensionId>`. Hard stop: `openclaw gateway stop`. All automations: `cron.enabled: false` or `OPENCLAW_SKIP_CRON=1`. Heartbeat off: `every: "0m"`. The employee's stop button is `automations disable` per job; list the ids in the runbook.
- **Cost:** `cost.turn_usd` in the status footer and `/status` (API-key models only; plan-billed sessions hide dollars); `openclaw gateway usage-cost --days N --agent <id>` aggregates from session logs; automations estimate from the agent's local `models.json` prices when the runtime reports tokens. Partial totals are not billing totals.
- **Memory:** plain Markdown in the workspace; "the model only remembers what gets saved to disk". `memory_search` (hybrid vector plus keyword), `openclaw memory status | search | index`, a pre-compaction memory flush, and a nightly **dreaming** pass that promotes daily notes into `MEMORY.md` (the synthesize verb, built in; the CEO still arbitrates by editing the file).
